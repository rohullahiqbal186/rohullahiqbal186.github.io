Autonomous Vehicle Safety Enhancement System (AVSES)

My bachelor's thesis: a deep learning system that recognizes drivers' hand gestures and rear signal lights in rear-view video, so that a following vehicle can tell early what the car ahead intends to do.

Status
Bachelor's thesis (final year project), BS Software Engineering, UAJK
My role
Designed and implemented the system; built and annotated the dataset
Methods
CNN for spatial features, LSTM for temporal modelling
Presented
Workshop for AI Explain Your Science

Problem

Driver-assistance and autonomous systems usually read a vehicle's intentions from its indicator and brake lights. In informal traffic, drivers often signal with a hand out of the window instead of, or as well as, the lights. A system that only watches the lights misses those signals.

The question for the thesis was whether a model could recognize these hand gestures, together with rear signal indicators, from video taken behind the vehicle.

Data

I built a custom dataset for the project: video from real-world driving scenarios, annotated for drivers' hand gestures and rear signal indicators.

The dataset contains real road footage, so it isn't published here.

Method

A gesture is a movement, not a pose, so a single frame isn't enough. The model has two parts:

  • A CNN extracts spatial features from each frame.
  • An LSTM reads the sequence of frame features and classifies the gesture or signal.

The illustration at the top of the home page is a loose picture of the same idea.

Outcome

The thesis produced the annotated dataset and a CNN-LSTM recognition system for hand gestures and rear signals. I later taught a workshop on the project for AI Explain Your Science.
